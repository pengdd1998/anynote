# AnyNote Design Context

## Users

General privacy-aware individuals who value personal privacy in their digital lives. They use AnyNote for personal journaling, secure note-taking across devices, and storing sensitive thoughts. They are not necessarily technical — they choose AnyNote because privacy should be simple and human, not because they understand XChaCha20-Poly1305.

**Context of use:** Quick capture of thoughts on mobile (commuting, in bed), extended writing sessions on desktop, occasional content publishing to social platforms. The app is used in intimate, personal moments — late-night journaling, morning reflections, private idea capture.

## Brand Personality

**Warm, private, elegant**

- **Warm**: Privacy feels human, not cold/technical. The app feels like a trusted companion, not a vault. Soft edges, gentle transitions, inviting interactions.
- **Private**: The UI respects the user's space. No unnecessary chrome, no data-heavy dashboards, no notification noise. Quiet confidence in its security.
- **Elegant**: Refined restraint. Every element is intentional. Never cluttered, never overwhelming. Like a well-made leather journal — simple but exceptional.

**Emotional goals:** Users should feel safe, calm, and unhurried. The interface should feel like opening a private journal, not launching an app.

## Aesthetic Direction

**Visual tone:** Soft sophistication with warmth. Think premium stationery, not tech startup. Subtle textures, warm neutrals with muted color accents, generous whitespace.

**Theme:** Both light and dark modes, system-adaptive.

- **Light mode:** Warm white surfaces (not clinical #fff), soft cream undertones, warm grey text. Like writing on high-quality paper.
- **Dark mode:** Deep navy/charcoal (not pure black), warm grey surfaces, muted accent glow. Like writing by lamplight.

**Color philosophy:** Warm neutrals with coral-amber accents. All neutrals tinted warm (slight red-orange hue). Accent color used sparingly for interactive elements and status.

**Typography:** SF Pro Display for platform-native feel. Consider pairing with a more distinctive display font for headings/brand moments — something warm and slightly editorial.

**References:**
- Bear Notes (warmth, simplicity, gentle Markdown)
- Day One journal (elegance, personal feel, beautiful typography)
- Notion's clean writing experience (without the workspace complexity)

**Anti-references:**
- NOT an enterprise/admin dashboard — no dense data grids, no metric cards, no form-heavy layouts
- NOT a generic chat/social app — no bright playful colors, no notification badges everywhere
- NOT standard Material/Apple Notes template — should feel intentional and distinctive

## Design Principles

1. **Quiet confidence** — The UI gets out of the way. No visual noise, no unnecessary decorations. Content is the star. Every pixel earns its place.

2. **Warm privacy** — Privacy is communicated through warmth, not through cold technical aesthetics. The app feels like a safe space, not a fortress. Soft colors, gentle motion, human touch.

3. **Elegant restraint** — Fewer elements, executed with precision. One beautiful interaction beats five mediocre ones. Generous whitespace, deliberate typography, considered color.

4. **Writing-first** — The interface serves writing and note-taking above all else. The editor experience is sacred — full-screen focus, minimal chrome, beautiful typography for reading.

5. **Platform-native warmth** — Embrace Material 3 on Android and native patterns on iOS, but add warmth on top. Not a generic Material app, not a counter-platform statement — the best of both worlds.
